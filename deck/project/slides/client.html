This is a real proposal from February 2026, with the client anonymized. The owner runs four ice cream stores (three open, one opening) plus a fast-growing catering business, and the franchisor offers little multi-unit support. In discovery he described tracking 170 flavors, about 40 active, with a daily visual count and a spreadsheet that averages two weeks of usage to set production against par levels; it didn’t sync between stores. He wanted staff to understand the why behind each number, so the batch sheet shows its math and allows overrides. The plan: three phases over 7–8 weeks on the Microsoft tools he already had, each ending in a go/no-go decision, piloting in one store before rollout, with success measured on the three targets shown. What happened next: in March he built his own version with AI in about two weeks, covering inventory, pricing and multi-store dashboards. My takeaway is that build-versus-buy has shifted: deep operating knowledge plus AI now beats a year-long vendor project, and the consulting value moves to scoping, data definitions and adoption.
